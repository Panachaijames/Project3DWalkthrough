## 2. Items that need a decision

riser 1. Built as R-8.5 says. Riser 1's middle, the second point of `walk` and the reader's `floorEnd`, is (20.66,
21.16): 0.26 m from RA-9's point (asserted). **Waiting:** `test_r2_drawings.py::test_ra9_the_house_bottom_as_ra9_writes_it`
(a strict xfail). **Proposed:** RA-9 reads "riser 1's middle within 0.3 m of (20.40, 21.15)"; then RI removes the xfail.

### D-2 The bank's north stair stays as lines because Level 2 has no floor at its top

It reads completely (18 risers on both levels, a direction from the cut, 0.206 m). Its top lies at (−0.5, 12.2), outside
Level 2's plate0, so by R-7.6 it stays as lines: "its top does not stand on a floor plate" (check). RA-9 allows it
("built or flagged"). Staff can floor the area ("Floor here") and it then climbs. No change proposed; said here because
the flag is a `check` on a building whose main stair climbs.

### D-3 Four `info` flags on the house for groups of three stair-layer lines

R-7.6: "every other stair group stays as lines and gets a `stair` flag ... also a group of stair lines that forms no

## 3. How R2 read the design where it was silent or loose (information; accepted unless RI says otherwise)

* **N-1 Treads are counted as parts** (R0.md issue 1): `treads` = tread parts, `landings` = landing parts; the bank's
  21 risers are 17 treads and 3 landings, the house's 19 and 1.
* **N-2 One riser from several lines (R-7.1 points 2, 3).** Lines of one 2° band are one riser when they are 5 cm apart
  or less and overlap by 60 % of the shorter, or lie on one line (1 cm) end to end (5 cm). The riser stands at the
  middle of the outermost of its long lines. Where two lines of a pair run side by side, a line that only runs on from
  the pair's end is not the riser (a string of the next flight, a landing's edge): the reference building's riser 7
  would otherwise be 2.44 m long.
* **N-3 Lanes (R-7.2 point 1).** A riser joins the lane it overlaps by 60 % of the shorter of the two, if it is 0.4 –
  1.67 times the lane's width; the lane's span is the median of its risers' ends; the usual lengths seed the lanes. A
  line longer than that which covers 60 % of a lane with two or more risers is that lane's riser too, cut to the lane
  (R-7.1 point 6: the house's landing edge at y 23.05). So a riser cut to half by a break line stays, and a short second
  line makes no lane of its own. "A chain is split only by a clear gap" needed no code: a lane never holds two spans.
* **N-4 The side edges of flared steps are no flight.** The house's three bottom steps are flared; their side edges
  (x 21.05, 21.35, 21.65) are three parallel lines 0.30 m apart. A flight whose every line starts at the end of a
  different riser of one other flight running across it is dropped.
* **N-5 Deeper end steps (R-7.1 point 5).** A going of 0.37 – 0.45 m is taken only as the first or the last step of a
  chain; in the middle it ends the chain.
* **N-6 The continuation (R-7.1 point 4)** also takes stair-layer lines the flight did not use; a continued riser on
  another layer counts as drawn beyond the cut (R-7.4 rule 3).
* **N-7 Turns (R-7.3).** L: the end risers' nearest ends within 0.4 m (the bank's west flight and Level 2's flight 4 are
  0.28 m apart at the corner). Straight: a gap of 0.45 m to 1.5 widths; with a sense unknown it needs side lines drawn
  along the landing. U: the two lanes 0.6 m apart or less (the bank's `DOWN` / `UP` pair is 0.73 m apart), the ends
  within 1.2 m of each other along the going, the landing out to a drawn line 0.5 – 3.0 m beyond, else to the first
  wall, else as deep as the median width. Where a U could turn at either end: the end with a drawn landing line before
  the one with only a wall; the landing drawn beyond the cut (hidden or on a stair-above layer) before the one drawn
  below it; then the end at which a flight is drawn beyond the cut. Still two: not chained.
* **N-8 The cut (R-7.4 rule 3)** is read from the first and the last riser of the chain; when they say the same, the
  flight a zig-zag break line crosses starts at this floor if it is the chain's first or last (the tower: every riser is
  in a hidden linetype).
* **N-9 Arrowheads (R-7.4 rule 2)**: two strokes of 0.08 – 0.45 m meeting at the end of a line, 10 – 70° back from it.
  Solid triangles are not read. A dot at the other end is not needed.
* **N-10 Row 1 of R-7.5 also without a word**: the upper plan's stair is the top of the stair from below when it joins
  it and its own floor end (by arrow or cut) is its far end. The house's upper plan is read that way (it adds no riser).
* **N-11 The upper plan's drawing of a stair that stays as lines gets a row of its own** on its floor, with the same
  reason and the two plans' count: `cut_slab` reads the kept void from the rows of the floor (R1.md section 5), so
  today's opening needs a row there. One stair, two `stair` flags (the house's ST-02, the tower's stair B, the bank's
  north stair).
* **N-12 The reasons, in this order** (the first that holds): kept by staff; no flight ("turning or curved steps"); a
  stair that arrives with nothing below it ("leads to a floor that is not in the model" on the lowest floor, else "its
  two floors' drawings do not line up"); the top floor ("leads to a floor that is not in the model"); the riser: up to
  0.30 m, or under 0.14 m, "N steps for H m give R m each"; over 0.30 m "its N steps would rise about ...: no floor of
  the model is at that height"; no direction; off the plate (top, then bottom). "The floors do not line up" is never
  raised for floors that are in fact misaligned: nothing tells the builder that.
* **N-13 "A storey of a–b m would fit ..."** is always added to "the riser does not fit" (the builder does not know
  whether the levels were typed or assumed): this stair's window, cut down by the climbable stairs between the same
  floors; "this stair" in place of "every stair between these floors" when they have no storey in common.
* **N-14 A stair that arrives and has no stair below it is not built from the upper plan alone.**
* **N-15 Which stair leaves.** A stair with a known floor end and no word, on a floor with a floor above, leaves it
  (R-7.5 row 3). A stair on the top floor that is not the top or the picture of one from below "leads to a floor that
  is not in the model", whatever its direction.
* **N-16 `pairs`**: a stair that passes a floor (the split level) joins the pairs it passes: no `stairs-none` for them.
* **N-17 The well (R-8.2)** is the union of the treads (without the lap) and landings that need headroom, grown by
  0.05 m with square corners, cut along riser n over 1 m beyond it. On a floor the stair passes it is grown all round.
  **The fenced band**: a piece of the upper plan's stair group (its hull) on the plate that is not well, wall, kept
  void or arrival, 0.2 – 8 m², touching the well, with 1 m (or a quarter of its edge) along railings; the bank's Level 2
  band is added (the well there is 7.1 m²).
* **N-18 Today's opening over a stair that stays as lines (R-4.3)**: the group's hull once per group; where a climbable
  stair arrives in the group, only the row's own footprint.
* **N-19 Clash cuts (R-8.3)**: one cut per part and per flight or landing over it, with that flight's lowest underside
  (the fixture maker makes one per part; on the reference building both give the one cut wall, 0.12 m² at 2.062 m). On
  the bank: the toilet's wall under the second landing (1.44 m), flight 3 (1.41 m), the third landing (1.96 m) and
  flight 4 (2.29 m), and one lintel. Railings cannot be cut (R1.md N-13).
* **N-20 The walking line** leaves riser 1 and riser n square to them (a flared first step's middle is not the
  flight's middle); its ends are moved toward the stair in 5 cm steps, never nearer than 0.3 m.
* **N-21 Not clipped**: treads are not clipped to the stair group's outline buffered by 0.05 m (R-8.1); a riser's ends
  are its own or its lane's. A curved first step (R-7.1 point 7) is not read: arcs are not riser lines.
* **N-22 More than the contract's keys**: a `StairFound` row carries `more` (R2's working data: the chain, what gave
  the direction), its flights `inserted` and `beyond` (one flag per riser). `StairBuilt.wellOf` can hold a floor the
  stair passes. `Stairway.direction` is one of `word`, `arrow`, `cut`, `floors`, `staff`.
* **N-23 Within 1 mm** (the contract test): the drawings draw nosing pairs at whole millimetres (a pair's middle can be
  0.5 mm off the definition) and the model rounds to millimetres, so the test allows 1 mm per coordinate.

## 5. What the other steps must know

* **R1.** Nothing was needed from R1's modules. `stairs_build.py` imports `slabs.arrival_of` (the arrival patch, for the
  fenced band). A `left` row can stand on an upper floor for a stair whose other row is on the floor below (N-11).
  `wellOf` can name a floor between `from` and `to`. `linesUsed` has the stair's two floors.
* **R3.** `stair` flags: one per `left` row, so one stair can have two (N-11) and a group with no flight has one (D-3).
  Texts hold an en dash. `stairClash` can have several rows for one part (N-19). `stairs-none` comes from
  `X.new_rule['pairs']` (N-16). The bank's model: `S1` and three `left` rows; the house's: `S1` and seven; the tower's:
  `S1` and two.
* **R4.** The house's tread 1 is a trapezoid 0.40 m deep (the flared step), tread 2 a trapezoid too; its walking line
  crosses the lower flight at a slant (riser middles x 20.66 → 20.36). The bank's stair is 0.90 m wide with three
  0.15 m landings, and Level 2 keeps its floor over flight 1 and the first landing. Landings are 0.15 m thick; the
  last tread laps 0.05 m under the slab; `walk[-2]` is the top riser's middle.
* **RI.** D-1's strict xfail. The new-rule models of the bank, the house and the tower now hold a stairway, treads,
  landings, wells and cut walls. R2 has no test marked `acceptance`.
