## 2. Items that need a decision

### D-1 The box proxy (R-9.9) stays unbuilt: no frame over 33 ms on the tower with the laptop's GPU

R-9.9: the box proxy "is used only if the frame time in Edge on this laptop goes over 33 ms on the tower". Measured by
`stairs.spec.ts` with `WT_FPS_GPU=1` (headless Edge started without the software-drawing flags: it draws with
"ANGLE (Intel, Intel(R) Iris(R) Xe Graphics (0x000046A8) Direct3D11 vs_5_0 ps_5_0, D3D11)"), Walk on the stair of the
tower's new-rule version, ceilings on, furniture shown, standing on riser 1 and then holding W up the flight (the body
rays against both floors): **60.1 fps standing and 60.0 fps walking** (`lib/fps.ts` `measureFps`, 5 s each), and while
W was held 301 frames in 5 s, the 95th percentile **16.8 ms**, the longest **17.0 ms**, **none over 33 ms** (the walker
climbed 0.00 -> 2.00 m, onto the mid-flight landing, in that time). The 60 fps is the display's frame cap, so the
frame's own cost is below 16.6 ms. The same on the house (RA-14's other version): 60.1 / 60.2 fps, longest frame
17.1 ms; and the bank: 60.2 / 60.2 fps, longest 17.4 ms; none over 33 ms. Three runs gave the same figures within
0.2 fps. **Proposed:** the box proxy is not built; RA-14's "≥ 30 fps" holds on the house and the tower.

In the software-drawn Edge of the test runs (SwiftShader) the same places run at 2.3 - 3.1 fps on the tower, 5.4 -
7.5 on the house, 6.3 - 9.7 on the bank and 7 - 15 on the synthetic files (depending on the machine's load): the
drawing, not the walker (a whole climb's 200 probe moves run inside one call there).

### D-2 RA-8's "a ray up ... hits a ceiling mesh at the ceiling height" meets the underside, 2 cm under it

The viewer draws a ceiling as its underside only (R4.md N-3), at `level + z0`, and `z0` is the ceiling height less the
2 cm plate. So the ray meets the house's ground-floor ceiling at 3.88 m (0.90 + 2.98, of 3.00 typed) and the upper
floor's at 7.68 m (4.80 + 2.88, of 2.90 typed); the bank, the tower and the reference building likewise. The spec
compares with the part's `z0` within 0.01 m. **Proposed:** RA-8 reads "at the ceiling's underside (the height less the
2 cm plate)".

### D-3 The GPU measure starts a second Edge inside the locked run

Playwright refuses `test.use({ launchOptions })` inside a describe group ("it forces a new worker"), and the frozen
config starts every browser with `--use-angle=swiftshader --enable-unsafe-swiftshader`. So the `WT_FPS_GPU=1` test
launches its own headless Edge (`chromium.launch({ channel: 'msedge' })`, flags `--allow-file-access-from-files
--ignore-gpu-blocklist`, `WT_FPS_GPU_ARGS` to change them) for its 15 seconds, while the run's own browser waits idle,
both under the run's one Edge lock. **Proposed:** accepted as the "script" RA-14 asks for; RI's acceptance runner reads
the figure from `<WT_TMP>/stairs/<file>.json` (`fpsGpu`).

### Decided by the main session (11 Nov 2026, between batches)

* **D-1: accepted.** The box proxy is not built: with the laptop's GPU no frame on the tower, the house or the bank
  passed 17.4 ms (none over 33 ms). R-9.9's condition is not met; the figures stay in section 4 as its evidence.
* **D-2: accepted.** RA-8 reads "a ray up hits the ceiling's underside, at the ceiling height less the 2 cm plate"; RI
  writes it into ACCEPTANCE.md.
* **D-3: accepted, for RI.** The acceptance runner reads the GPU figure from `stairs/<file>.json` (`fpsGpu`); the second
  Edge that the GPU test starts stays inside the locked run.

## 3. How R7 read the design where it was silent or loose (information; accepted unless RI says otherwise)

* **N-1 The climb** (RA-10 Up / Down) follows R4.md section 5 exactly: `goToFloor(from)`, the position written to
  `walk[0]`, then only probe calls; each piece's heading from where the walker is; `probe.walk(deg, 0.05)` once per move
  (the same moves as one `probe.walk(deg, len)`, so the foot can be read after every move: the largest rise and drop
  per 5 cm move are recorded and judged against 0.25 / 0.30 m); `probe.crosses` asked at each piece's start, from where
  the walker stands to the piece's end point (R4.md D-1, decided); then 1.0 m on along the line's last direction, with
  no `crosses` there (a wall may stand within that metre: the bank's walker stops 0.25 - 0.30 m into it). Down starts at
  `walk[n-1]` on the upper floor and ends at `walk[0]`. "0.5 m or more past the last riser" is measured from the top
  riser's middle (`walk[n-2]`) along the line's last direction (down: from riser 1's middle, `walk[1]`).
* **N-2 Floor names**: the climb judges the floor named at the end (`s.floor`), the last public `modechange` event and
  the floor button's `aria-pressed`; `modechange` also fires for the mode entry and the `goToFloor` of the start.
* **N-3 The keyboard start**: Walk (key 2), the position written to `walk[0]`, the yaw set toward `walk[1]`, W held
  until `state.foot` has risen three risers (polled every 50 ms; the frame rate is not judged), then `stairWalks` with
  `start: 'here'` goes on from the segment of the line nearest to the walker.
* **N-4 "A door probe beside the stair, then a far one"**: the doors and passages of the stair's lower floor, sorted by
  distance from `walk[0]`, are probed on the fresh page; the nearest and the farthest that have a start spot on the
  floor are kept (the house's garden gate `ground-034`, with floor on neither side, tells nothing); then a walk that
  ends three risers up the stair (R-9.8: "two treads up a stair would leave a stale foot"), then the near door, then the
  far one. Pass: the same answers as on the fresh page, and the foot on the floor's level at the first spot written.
* **N-5 The climbs and the probes run with every furniture kind hidden**, as the structure checks are judged
  (`structure.spec.ts`, `upload-walk.spec.ts`); the frame-rate test keeps the furniture shown (R-9.9's load: both
  floors' boxes), and its W walk climbed with it on all three real versions (house 1.09 -> 2.20 m, bank 0 -> 0.71 m,
  tower 0 -> 2.00 m in 5 s with the GPU).
* **N-6 The ceiling checks** run on every file with ceiling parts: per floor a point in a closed space with `ceiling`
  under a ceiling part (not over its holes), a ray up from 1.2 m above the level against every ceiling mesh: the first
  one met must be `<floor> ceilings`, shown, at `level + z0` (D-2); every ceiling mesh hidden in Orbit, shown in Ghost
  and in Walk; the tick (label "Ceilings") hides them in Walk and shows them again. A file without ceiling parts must
  have no tick. "The old versions' panels show no Ceilings tick" is checked on the five seeded versions with this
  viewer's template (no tick, no ceiling mesh, no `stairways`).
* **N-7 RA-6 in `upload-walk.spec.ts`** (`WT_ROOMS_PROBES=1` or a key's path): a file is judged only when its model has
  `stairways` and it is a drawing of the key (the key's project id, or the same source file names as the drawing's
  `*-rooms` settings fixture); each probe is found again at its sheet point less the model's `offset` (an opening of
  its floor, or a glass probe, within 0.1 m; the model point is the sheet point less the model's `offset` and less the
  floor's `sheetShift`, the house's upper plan standing 74.022 m to the right on its one sheet); for each side that had
  no floor: floor at some of 0.4, 0.5 ... 0.9 m in
  front (must for the key's `must` drawings), and the walk from the first start spot with room is recorded (a door:
  metres past its line; glass: metres short of it) for `GAPS.md`. `ground-034` is recorded, not judged (R1.md D-2).
  Without `WT_ROOMS_PROBES` the spec is the upload milestone's.
* **N-8 `wallWalks` on a new-rule version** (the scaffold's helper, unchanged) asks `probe.crosses` from where the walk
  ended, not from its start (R4.md D-1). A random walk that climbs onto a flight and ends above a wall cut under it could
  count "went through"; none did in the runs of section 4. If RI sees one on a stair, that is the cause.
* **N-9 `probe.ground()` and `foot()`** are read at the end of each climb: the ground under the walker equals the foot
  on every file (no walker left in the air or inside a step).
