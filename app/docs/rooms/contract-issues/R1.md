## 2. Items that need a decision

### D-1 The bank's new-rule model has 23 names, not 18 (RA-7 against R-3.2)

RA-7 says the bank's new-rule version "shows 18 labels at the drawing's positions ... and its version counts 18 rooms,
as today". R-3.2 says short names are kept (2 letters or more, not a stair word, not a tag) so that `BAR`, `BOH.`, `WC`

records** (L1 12, L2 11) and the version would count 23 rooms. The 18 of today are all there, at the drawing's
positions, and 18 of 18 stand in the room that carries their name (RA-3's must holds).

No letter count keeps `BAR` and drops `ATM`. **Built as R-3.2 writes it.** Proposal: RA-7's count for the bank becomes
23 (the 18 of today plus `ATM` x 3, `BA`, `BE`). The other way is a longer limit for names with no tag line, which loses
`BAR`, `BOH.` and `WC` on the new suite. In the tests: `test_r1_drawings.py::test_ra7_the_bank_shows_18_labels` is a
strict xfail that names D-1; `test_ra7_the_banks_labels_as_built` pins the 23.

## 3. How R1 read the design where it was silent or loose (information; accepted unless RI says otherwise)

* **N-1 The pocket rule (R-2.5).** "Shrink it by 1.5 m and grow it back. What comes back is open outside. What does not
  come back is a pocket" read literally finds only nooks under 3 m across: a 10 x 10 m room with a 0.8 m opening
  comes back whole and would not be flagged, against RA-3. The trial the design cites (`pockets.py`) does this, and so
  does the builder: the space with no floor is shrunk by 1.5 m; a piece of it that no longer reaches the frame is cut
  off from the outside (its way out is under 3 m wide); the pocket is that piece grown back, with the corners and nooks
  beside it that a 1.5 m disc from outside cannot reach. An area with one mouth ends on the straight line across it.
* **N-2 Specks (R-2.2 step 1).** A piece is free-standing when it is its own piece of the network after the cracks
  are closed. Under 0.05 m2 filled it is a speck, unless it is a lone drawn line: thin as a 2 cm strip (mean width up
  to 3.5 cm) and 0.3 m or longer. So a short single-line part (6 cm wide, under 0.83 m) is a speck; a drawn line of
  0.3 m or more is not.
* **N-3 A bridge that needs a second pass.** The strip joins the two nearest points of the parts, often two corner
  tips, which a 2 cm strip meets in a point (the 10 cm crack rule does not join corners that meet diagonally: tower
  L30 lost 145 m2 that way). So every closing has a 3 cm pad at each end in the network, and the bridge rule runs
  again on its own result until no piece lies between two open areas (three rounds at most; one is the rule).
  The end-to-face doorway test (R-2.6) is run on the working copy of the network, and only for the candidates that
  lie on the floor the candidates gain together (the others cannot lose any): 112 ring computations became a
  handful on the new suite (27 s to 2 s), with the same closings.
* **N-4 X marks.** (a) A line is a diagonal of one X only, the best-fitting box first: on the tower the lift-door X
  and the `A-DETL` X of one lift also pair across each other into four larger boxes, which R-4.3 forbids ("never joined
  into a larger shape"). (b) Diagonals that cross at less than 30 degrees still mark a slot when the other tests pass;
  it is a `mark`, reported and never cut. Without this the house's duct and roof slots, which RA-5 wants listed, are
  not X marks at all (their diagonals cross at 11 - 17 degrees). (c) There is no dimension role, so the lines of
  dimension layers are read; the drawn-outline test keeps them out. (d) A "+" (two lines crossing at right angles in
  their middles) marks a diamond when its outline happens to be drawn; on the tower three such lie inside the lift
  X's and change nothing. (e) The rectangle may be rotated (the frame is tried along the sheet's axes first, then
  along the lines between the ends).
* **N-5 An unfloored area that is mostly stair is not flagged** (half or more of it inside the hull of the floor's
  stair lines). It is listed in `slab.dropped` ("mostly stair"), as today's rule lists such strips. Without this the
  bank's Level 2 gets a `slab-gap` flag over its outside stair (4.5 m2).
* **N-6 A room's outline reaches the drawn lines.** A face ends on the inner edge of the 2 cm strips; the room is the
  face grown by 1 cm, so its area is the area between the wall faces (the reference building's rooms come out at the
  fixture's 57.9, 19.7 and 57.6 m2; without it 57.6, 19.6, 57.3). The ring is not shrunk: it is 1 cm outside the wall
  faces all round (0.3 - 0.5 m2 on the reference building).
* **N-7 Enclosed shapes.** An enclosed shape (R-3.1) is joined to the face across its longest loose edge (the three
  longest are tried); a free-standing object's own faces are counted as enclosed shapes too; a building standing
  free inside a room (a core with rooms of its own) is cut out of that room's outline, so a point stands in one room.
* **N-8 `source` of a space**: `staff` when a staff closing line lies on its edge, `outline` when half or more of its
  edge runs along a closed room outline, else `network`. An area staff marked "Floor here" is floor (and gets a
  ceiling, R-5.2) but is no room: like a terrace it has no `spaces[]` record, and a name typed with it names nothing.
* **N-9 "The probe points stand in rooms" (RA-3, tower).** The point 0.4 - 0.9 m in front of a glass probe lies in the
  curtain wall's own band (on the plate); the room starts 0.9 - 1.5 m in. The test asks for a room within 2.5 m in
  front of the opening, and for the plate at some point of 0.4 - 0.9 m.
* **N-10 "No floor here" is taken out of plate0 and is also a hole.** R-4.1 takes it out in pass 1, R-4.3 lists it as
  a hole of pass 3: both are done, so `slab.plate` is the plate without it and `voids` carries a `no floor` record.
* **N-11 Report rows are in the floor's own sheet frame** (the build frame less the alignment shift), as every row of
  the upload milestone's report: the house's upper floor is 74.022 m off the ground plan's frame. Fix points are read
  in the same frame. The tracer wrote build-frame points; on drawings with no shift (the reference building) nothing
  differs.
* **N-12 Rows that carry more than the fixtures' keys** (the lists are untyped): `slab.unfloored` + `kind`
  (`gap` | `pocket` | `fenced`); `slab.closings` of staff lines + `fix` (the index in `fixes.closings`); `rooms.open`
  rows of the second kind of R-6.1 ("a closed area of N m2 with no door") are `{ name, at, space, area, why: 'no
  door' }`; `slab.dropped` also lists the fenced areas with no door, the stair areas and today's dropped strips that
  the new plate does not floor either, each with its reason. A closing's two ends are in a fixed order (the lower by
  x, then y, first), so the same closing has the same row in every build.
* **N-13 The railings are not among `FloorBuilt.parts`** (they are added after the plate is known), so no
  `StairCut` can name one, although R-8.3 lists railings among the parts a stair cuts.
* **N-14 Slab pieces of 4 m2 or less** are listed in `slab.dropped` from 0.01 m2 up (slivers under that are not
  rows), after the holes are cut again (R-4.4's order).
* **N-15 The arrival patch.** `StairBuilt.arrival` is a point (the top of the walking line). The 1.2 m of floor past
  the top riser that no hole may take (R-4.3, R-8.2) is built from the stairway's last walking segment and its width
  (5 cm more on each side). A hole that takes none of it is left exactly as it was found.
* **N-16 A name with a tag line below it is a name whatever it reads** (as today: the bank's `BOH` has "Not Enclosed"
  under it). The 2-letter and tag tests of R-3.2 apply to texts with no tag line.
* **N-17 `slab.holes` rows that are not cut** (`cut: false`, with `why`): `the lowest floor` (an X void there: the
  `hole-skipped` flag, info); `over 60 m2`, `over 30 % of the floor` (`hole-skipped`, check); and rows that are only
  listed and raise no flag: `what: 'X mark'` with `narrower than 0.6 m`, `under 1 m2` or `its lines cross at less
  than 30 degrees`; a `stair` or X hole `under 0.5 m2`; an X `off the floor plate`.

## 5. What the other steps must know

* **R2.**
  * `network_of`'s result has the contract's six keys and one more, `more` (R1's working data; do not rely on it);
    `net['N']` is the network with every closing in it; `net['faces']` is every face of 1 m2 or more, `net['spaces']`
    the rooms. `plate0[key]` already leaves out the "no floor here" rooms.
  * `cut_slab` cuts `wellOf[key]` whole (never dropped for its size), `keptVoid` from 0.5 m2 and only on a floor that
    is not the lowest, and takes the arrival patch (N-15) of every climbable stair that arrives at the floor out of
    every other hole. The patch needs `stairway['walk']` to end with the middle of the top riser and then the top, and
    `stairway['width']`.
  * `apply_cuts` takes the lower `z1` where two cuts overlap on one part; a cut names a part by its index in
    `FloorBuilt.parts` as they are before any cut (N-13: no railings).
  * With `stairs: 'climb'`, `model.stairOutlines` and the summary's `stairs` block are made from `StairsResult`: one
    group per stair built from the floor and one per `left` row of the floor; a `left` row's flights and senses are
    read from the `find_stairs` row with the same `id`, when there is one.
  * `linesUsed[key]` are indices into `FloorBuilt.old_stairs['lines']` (the floor's `stair` lines, then its
    `stair-above` lines), as the tracer's.
* **R3.**
  * The row shapes of N-12 and N-17; N-11 (the floor's own sheet frame; the tracer's rows were in the build frame).
  * A `rooms.open` row with `space` is a room over 150 m2 with no door, not a name.
  * The whole floor's flag ("walls that never close", R-2.5) needs no new report key: `slab.plate` and the walls are
    there. `platesDropped` is empty with the new rule (`plate-dropped` is the old rule's flag).
  * `fixes.closings` and `fixes.rooms` rows are `{ fix, matched }` for every fix of the settings, also one whose floor
    is not in the build (unmatched).
  * The bank's new-rule model has 23 `rooms[]` records (D-1): 23 labels in the top view and the Max script.
* **R5 / R6.** `propose_settings` writes `NEW_RULES` and carries `fixes.closings / rooms / stairs` over from a recipe.
  The builder fills a missing `use` / `slab` / `ceiling` of a new fix with `line` / `auto` / `auto`, reads
  `floorPast` as a bool, and refuses other values (`bad_settings`). A closing fix `use: 'none'` must carry the two ends
  of the closing as the report gives them (`slab.closings[].a / b`, within 0.3 m). "Close it here" on a `slab-gap`
  flag writes the mouth's two ends as a closing line: each end is extended to the network within 0.5 m.
* **RI.** D-1 - D-4. The new-rule reference models will move again when R2's stairs land (wells, treads, clash cuts,
  ceilings' headroom holes).
