## 2. Items that need a decision

### D-1 "`probe.crosses` along each segment is never true" (RA-10, the Edge part) holds only when asked from the segment's start

`crosses` is measured at chest height above the **foot** of the walker where it stands (R-9.2: "`crosses` ... measured
from the foot"). A wall piece cut under a flight (R-8.3) lies under the walking line by design: on the reference
building, asked from the ground floor, the piece of the line over the cut cupboard wall answers **true**
(`climb-modes.test.ts`). Asked with the walker standing at the start of each piece, it is false on the reference
building and on every piece of the house's, the bank's and the tower's lines (recorded by the integration test in
`real-climb-<name>.json`). **Proposed:** RA-10 reads "`probe.crosses` along each segment, asked when the walker stands at
the segment's start, is never true", and R7's `stairWalks` asks it there. A flight whose far end stands over a cut wall
taller than the chest height at the flight's start would still answer true: none of the four models has one.

### D-2 A tread or landing names its floor by the foot, not by the floor its part belongs to (R-9.5 against R-9.3)

R-9.5: Ghost to Walk "lands on the highest surface ... The floor becomes that surface's floor". Every tread is a part of
the lower floor, so a landing on the top tread would name the lower floor one riser under the upper slab, against "the
floor named matches the foot" (R-9.2, R-9.3). **Built:** on a plate the floor is the plate's floor (then `findFree`, as
today); on a tread or landing the floor is named from the foot (the nearest level, with the hysteresis). The same for a
double-click. **Proposed:** R-9.5 says so.

### D-3 The replay shows the ceilings for the moment of its last fade, in Orbit (R-9.7's two sentences)

"In the floor group's `late` list, so the replay shows them at the end like the slabs" and "Never in Orbit". Built as
both say: the ceiling meshes are in `late`; the replay's last fade makes every late object visible, and its end calls
`visibility.applyToggles()`, which hides the ceilings again (the replay runs in Orbit). They face down, so from the
replay's camera above the building nothing of them is drawn. If "never in Orbit" must hold for `mesh.visible` at every
frame (a browser check on the flag during the replay), the ceilings come out of `late`: one line in `core/build.ts`.

### Decided by the main session (11 Nov 2026, between batches)

* **D-1: accepted.** RA-10's "never true" for `probe.crosses` is read "asked when the walker stands at the segment's
  start"; R7 asks it there.
* **D-2: accepted as built.** A plate names its own floor; a tread or landing is named from the foot. RI writes that into
  R-9.5 together with the D-13 "Climbing" row (section 4).
* **D-3: accepted as built.** Ceilings stay in `late`; the replay's last fade sets them visible for a moment in Orbit
  and `applyToggles` hides them again. They face down, so nothing is drawn from above.

## 3. How R4 read the design where it was silent or loose (information; accepted unless RI says otherwise)

* **N-1 One mesh for treads and landings**, `<key> tread:storey`, with the `tread` style (R-9.9: "merged per floor into
  one mesh"). The `landing` style of a presentation is not used; the default colours are the same. A second mesh
  (`<key> tread:storey:<to>`) is made only when stairways of one floor rise to different floors (each scales with its own
  storey). The cut wall pieces are `<key> solid:storey`, colliders.
* **N-2 The meshes carry what the other modules need**: `userData.steps` (treads and landings), `userData.stairTo` (the
  stairway's upper floor, for `core/heights.ts`), `userData.ceiling` (for `core/visibility.ts`).
  `BuiltScene.toggleObjects` is frozen with three lists, so the ceilings are not in it.
* **N-3 A ceiling is its underside only**: one down-facing face at `level + z0`, not the 2 cm plate (R-5.3:
  "single-sided, facing down, so from above ... ceilings are not drawn"). No uv, no edges, no BVH.
* **N-4 Surfaces are listed only when the model has `stairways`** (the contract's comment); `walkables` are the slabs as
  before. Surfaces count whether the Floor plates tick shows them or not, as today's floor test (three.js rays do not
  read `visible`); `readouts.groundAt` therefore lost the scaffold's `visible` filter. `groundAt`'s default `h0` of
  3.0 m starts on the upper slab's top when the storey is exactly 3.0 m (the reference building): ask with a smaller
  `h0` for the lower floor's ground there.
* **N-5 The head ray is cast at the next centre from the NEW foot** (the ground just found). **The gap bridge moves the
  walker to the next centre, not to the bridged point**: its foot takes the height found 0.08 m further on.
* **N-6 The step window has a tolerance of 0.1 mm** (`STEP_EPS`): a riser of exactly 0.25 m is climbed.
* **N-7 The floors the body rays meet**: every floor of the stack with `level - slabThickness < foot + 2.0` and
  `level + wall height > foot`, strictly (0.1 mm), so the lower floor, whose wall top is the upper level, is not met on
  the upper plate. The wall height is the Build settings' value. On the three real models the wall height equals the
  storey: a plate meets one floor and the stair two. A lower floor whose walls are higher than its storey is met on the
  upper plate as well (its wall tops stand there).
* **N-8 `findFree` also wants head room** over the spot (it never starts the walker under a low flight), beside the
  ground under it that R-9.2 asks; the ground must be the floor's own plate.
* **N-9 The probe's rule (R-9.8) also covers `foot()` and `ground()`**, a written `s.floor`, and the frame: a frame in
  Walk that finds the walker not where it last put itself starts it from the floor's level too (the browser keeps
  ticking between a test's write and its probe call; either way the result is the same). So a test that writes
  `s.pos.x / z` (or `s.floor`) always gets the floor's level, and a test that wants the walker on a tread walks it there
  with `probe.walk`.
* **N-10 `probe.walk` returns the horizontal distance and still goes in whole 5 cm moves**: `walk(deg, 1.68)` makes 34
  moves, 1.70 m (as before the milestone).
* **N-11 Build settings while walking**: when a level changes and the walker is in Walk, the foot goes to the surface
  nearest to where it was above its floor (on a tread it stays on the tread), without easing.
* **N-12 The eye easing** is `y += (foot + eye - y) x (1 - e^(-dt / 0.1))` a frame, snapped when under 0.5 mm. A teleport
  (mode entry, the floor buttons, PgUp / PgDn, the eye slider, a double-click, a landing, a probe call) writes the eye
  directly.
* **N-13 A model with the `stairways` key but no surface in a stack** is not floor-checked there (today's "no walkables,
  no check"). A part with `fit: 'storey'` whose `stair` names no stairway (the parser refuses it) would be `fixed` or
  `scale`.
* **N-14 The shared test context** (`test/harness/context.ts`, naive on purpose) lists the slabs and one single-sided
  mesh per tread or landing as surfaces and draws no ceilings: enough to walk a v2 model there. The real meshes, the
  storey scaling and the ceilings are tested on the real core (`test/unit/core/build-v2.test.ts`,
  `test/unit/modes/climb-dom-kit.ts`).
* **N-15 The test hook** gives `FL[key].surfaces` (the contract's optional field).
* **N-16 The box proxy (R-9.9) was not built.** Its condition is a frame over 33 ms in Edge on the tower, which R4 did
  not measure in a browser (R7's scripted measure does). In Node a move on the stair, with the body rays against both
  floors, costs about 50 microseconds on the house, the bank and the tower (20 climbs up and down each), and the first
  view's CPU part of the tower's new-rule model (parse, checks, `buildScene`) takes 59 - 88 ms (seven runs), against
  RA-14's 350 ms.

## 4. The D-13 "Climbing" row (for RI to add to `../DESIGN.md` 13; the existing rows do not change)

```

```

## 5. What the other steps must know

* **R7 (viewer browser tests).**
  * Meshes: `<key> tread:storey` (treads and landings, `userData.steps`), `<key> solid:storey` (cut walls),
    `<key> ceilings` (`userData.ceiling`; `visible` only in Walk and Ghost with the tick
    `input[data-wt=toggle][data-toggle=ceilings]`, label "Ceilings"; never in the floor's `colliders`, `surfaces` or
    `rise`). `window.__viewer`'s floors have `surfaces`.
  * Start a stair walk with `goToFloor(from)`, then write `s.pos.x / z` to `walk[0]`, then only probe calls: the first
    one puts the foot on the floor's level. **Never write the position between the pieces of the line** (it is a
    teleport to the level, N-9). Walk piece by piece with `probe.walk`, each heading taken from where the walker is now
    to the next point (`probe.walk` goes in whole 5 cm moves, N-10; `climb-dom-kit.ts` `to()` is that helper in Node).
  * Read the height with `probe.foot()`: the camera's y lags it by up to about 0.2 m in a frame-driven climb and equals
    foot + eye after a probe call. `probe.ground()` is the surface under the walker within the step window, or null.
  * `probe.crosses` for a piece of the line is asked while the walker stands at that piece's start (D-1).
  * The floor named changes 0.20 m past mid-storey; bus `mode`, the floor buttons' `aria-pressed`, the HUD and the
    public `modechange` follow in the same call. A mid-storey landing keeps the name it was reached with (the tower's,
    at 2.00 m of 4.00 m: L29 going up, L30 coming down).
  * The real stairs, walked in Node along `walk` (section 1): the house 0.90 to 4.80 m (its slanted lower flight and
    the 0.40 m first tread are climbed), the bank 0.00 to 3.70 m through its three landings, the tower 0.00 to 4.00 m.
    After the top the bank's walker stops 0.30 m into the extra metre (a wall), 1.30 m past the last riser.
  * A stair is climbable only while its riser is 0.25 m or less: Build settings that raise a storey past
    `0.25 x risers` stop the climb at the first riser (the stair still scales; tested with a 4.2 m storey).
* **R6 (staff app).** Nothing in the viewer's public API changed. `modechange` now also fires when the walker changes
  floor on a stair. A version with ceilings shows a Ceilings tick in the viewer's panel (`presentation.labels.ceilings`,
  default "Ceilings"), and `Viewer.setHeights` and the Build settings card scale stairs with the storey.
* **RI (integration).**
  * The D-13 row (section 4); D-1's wording in RA-10; D-2 and D-3.
  * The integration tests on the real models are in `packages/viewer/test/unit/modes/climb.test.ts`, defined only when
    `WT_ROOMS_MODELS` names a folder of `<name>.model.json` files (`WT_ROOMS_NAMES`, default `house,bank`):
