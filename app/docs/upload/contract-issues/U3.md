# Contract issues: U3, the model builder (upload milestone, 4 Nov 2026)

Written only by step U3 (docs/upload/WORK_BREAKDOWN.md U3). The integration step UI settles each item. "Information"
items say how U3 read a silent or loose spot of the design; "accepted" at integration means the design is read that way
from now on. Nothing here needs a frozen file changed for the bank: the port reproduces v2 byte for byte.

## 1. How the builder reports a failure (U1 to read; contract gap)

`contracts.build` has no error type, and U-3.4 / `RunErrorCode` need a code, a plain message and the step. U3 raises
`walkthrough_builder.build.errors.BuildError(code, message, step)` (also exported as `walkthrough_builder.build.BuildError`),
code one of `no_floors`, `bad_settings`, `area_empty`, `align_failed`, `detect_failed`, `build_failed`; `record()` gives
`JobRecord.error`'s `{code, message, step}`. Messages name floors and files by their names, never a path. `MemoryError`
and `RecursionError` are never wrapped (the step child exits 75, U-3.5). Any other exception out of `build` is
`build_failed`. **U1's step child should map `BuildError` by its `code`.** UI: confirm, or move the class into
`contracts.py`.

## 2. Grid labels whose `line` is only the bubble's leader (U2, UI)

U-9.4 gives a label to the floor whose area its `line` crosses. The scaffold's provisional reader
(`fixture_reader.drawing_read`, `fixtures/upload/drawing/sukhumvit.drawing.json`) gives the house's plan labels a 1.5 m
leader stub (y 30.04 to 28.54 for a plan ending at y 25.85): no line crosses a plan box, and auto alignment would fail.
U3 keeps the rule and adds a bounded continuation (`build/align.py assign_labels`): a line that crosses no area is
continued away from its bubble for up to 10 m, and the label goes to the FIRST area met along it (so the lettered
bubbles at x 73.7 go to the upper plan, not to the ground plan behind them). Elevation labels stay out (in the fixture
they have no line; with lines they run up into the elevations, away from the plans). Result on the fixture: the upper
floor's shift is (-74.0221, 0.0) m with a 0.9 mm spread (A-5: -74.021 +- 0.002), 16 shared labels in two families.
**ACCEPTANCE's "12 + 12 labels per floor"** cannot be checked on the provisional fixture: 27 of its 63 labels have no
line (the ground gets 19 labels, the upper 16). With U2's reader the test should be re-run (`test_u3_align.py`,
`test_u3_drawings.py::test_house_aligns_by_its_grid`); the rule needs no change when U2's lines are longer.

## 3. Additions to the typed result (U4; information)

* `FixesReport.setAside` rows carry one more key, `key`: the cache key of the item (or INSERT) the fix matched, or null.
  The overlay's `setAside` (upload-contracts.ts OverlayFloor) needs the keys actually removed, which differ from the
  ref's key when it matched by element id or sig. The TypedDict types the row as `dict`, so the walker accepts it.
* `doorHeadsFrom` values: `'name' | 'floor' | 'drawing' | 'default'` as typed, plus **`'fix'`** for an opening fix's
  head (U-10.6). The model's opening `headFrom` keeps the prototype's strings (`'name'`, `'most common'`, `'default'`)
  and gains `'floor'` and `'fix'` (the bank has neither: its bytes are unchanged).
* `report.summary.namedHeads` has string keys (`"2.4"`, as JSON writes float keys), so the summary equals
  `bank_summary.json` in memory as well as on disk.
* `ReplayFacts.counts` = lines per category as the replay draws them (noise: the longest 6,000), equal to the scaffold
  fixture's; `ReplayFloor.sheet` is rounded to 2 decimals as the bank did (the rest of the replay is unrounded model
  coordinates, as U-10.8 says). Floors sharing a sheet sit at `[source position + sheetShift]`.
* `BuildResult.seconds`: `read`, `align`, `detect:<key>`, `build`, `total`.
* Report positions (`singles[].a/b`, `railsLeftOut[].at`, `roomLabels[].at`, `platesDropped[].at`) are each floor's
  SHEET metres (its source's frame, before the alignment shift), rounded to 1 mm; `platesDropped[].at` is the plate's
  representative point (the scaffold fixture used the box centre). `swingsUnmatched[].at` is the swing's hinge (arc
  centre), sheet m. `railsLeftOut` lists the railings not built as thin bands and the stair rails off an upper floor
  (the bank's L2: 3).
* `FloorUploadReport.itemsNoPoints` is the number of the floor's SOURCE items that have no points at all (they belong
  to no floor). U-9.1's "the house 2,267" is in fact the number of house items outside both plan areas (33,051 - 30,784
  in the reference cache); the house has 0 items without points, as have the other five drawings.
* `subtreesOutside`: INSERT subtrees with no majority in any floor that were left out whole, counted on the floor that
  held most of their points (the suite: 3).

## 4. Readings where the design is loose (information)

* **Whole-subtree reading (U-9.1)**: an INSERT goes with its subtree to the floor whose area (box + pad) holds MORE THAN
  HALF of its subtree's drawn points (insertion and text points when it draws nothing). One with no majority is walked
  into when it holds other INSERTs (a container such as a view block or an xref straddling two plans: its own INSERT
  item is then read per item), else left out whole and counted. Under `wholeFile` both readings are one list (the bank
  cannot move: tested). Line readers keep the per-item reading.
* **`furniture.pieceLayer: 'drawn'`**: "the majority of what is drawn in it is on furniture layers" is read as the suite
  prototype read it: the layer most of its drawn items are on (ties by name) has the furniture role; a block holding
  an INSERT on a furniture layer, or one that is itself such a candidate, is walked into.
* **`furniture.looseLayers`**: "layers whose loose lines become boxes (suite)" is read as the suite prototype read it:
  lines on those layers drawn outside every block (depth 0). With no `looseLayers` the bank's rule stands (lines on the
  furniture layers not inside a piece, at any depth: in a Revit export everything sits inside the view block).
* **`parapets: 'auto'`**: "only where the floor's wall layers carry solid fills" is read as: the floor has at least one
  hatch on a wall or column layer (the bank's parapet rule itself tests against all wall fills, solid or not). The
  house has none, so no parapet is built there (tested).
* **Accepted outline edges (U-10.6 fix 3)**: walls 0.20 m on the side away from the room; glass 0.02 m (a pane), the
  same side. The thickness of an outline glass is not given by the design.
* **Opening fixes matched to a record whose kind is set to `none`**: the fix row's `matched` is the record's id (it did
  match); the record's `DetectorRecordOut.modelId` is null and its `fix` the fix's index.
* **Units**: the builder uses `settings.sources[i].units` (the staff's) and falls back to `SourceContext.units`; U1's
  `ensure` step makes the two agree.
* **Set-aside keys**: the matcher relies on U-7.2's key format (`/`-joined, an ATTRIB `<insert key>@<handle>`); an
  INSERT ref also removes the INSERT's attributes (`key@...`), not only `key/...`. The element INSERT of an item is the
  first `element_depth + 1` components of its key (scaffold note U0-6).

## 5. Acceptance figures that are "should" and are not met exactly (information)

* **drawnVariant (U-11.7, should)**: all 26 pieces in the 9 families of `furniture_test/bank_furnished/analysis.md`
  come out `mirrorY` (front to back, as the analysis says), and two more pieces the analysis does not list: one of the
  three wall-hung basins (`Wall-hung vitreous china basin-Meridian-327242xx0`, L2-sanitary-003: 0.2 mm mirrored in y
  against 0.37 m as drawn, a real mirror) and one printer turned 180 degrees (a near-tie of a near-symmetric drawing).
  The test checks the 26 and allows these 2.
* **The suite's furniture against its prototype's 120 (should: within 5 %, every kind present)**: 127 (+5.8 %), every
  kind present; plants 12, seats 14, sofas 7, curtains 21, lamps 9, tall cabinets 3, bed, TV as the prototype. The
  differences come from the design's own rules: blocks holding chairs are walked into under `pieceLayer: 'drawn'` (the
  study's meeting table with three chairs and the banquette with two armchairs become their parts), pieces keep the
  layer of their INSERT when that is a furniture layer (`'drawn'` applies only to INSERTs on other layers), and 34
  loose-shape boxes against the prototype's 30 (its suite area was the wall cluster + 2 m, the settings' box is the
  detector's). The three blocks on `A-4. Dim` the prototype took as furniture are on a layer the settings call `other`.
* **The house's upper floor**: 32 pieces against the prototype's 29 (ground 47 = 47, kind for kind): the prototype's
  `settle()` (rug / zone outlines that enclose a smaller piece, the inner of two nested outlines) is not among
  U-10.5's rules and is not ported.
