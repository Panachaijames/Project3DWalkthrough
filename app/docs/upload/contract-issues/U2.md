# Contract issues: U2 (upload milestone)

Written only by step U2, the reader and suggestions (docs/upload/WORK_BREAKDOWN.md U2), on 4 Nov 2026. The integration
step UI settles each item. Where an item is information only (how U2 read a silent or loose spot of the design, or a
measurement that came out differently), "accepted" means the design is read that way from now on. Numbers are from
`npm run test:builder` on the reference caches (WT_REF = `%TEMP%\wt-u0\ref`).

## 1. Answers to the scaffold's questions (U0.md)

* **U0-5, keys of repeated source handles.** The rule: a key part is the entity's own handle (top level), or the handle
  of the block entity it was copied from (`source_of_copy`); a level with no such handle is `~<index in
  virtual_entities order>`; a handle met a second time under the same INSERT gets `~<index>` appended (`2A`, then
  `2A~7`). The first occurrence keeps the bare handle. In ezdxf 1.4.4 `virtual_entities()` of a MINSERT yields only the
  first copy (its docstring says so; prims keeps only that copy too), so MINSERTs repeat nothing; repeats come from
  pieces ezdxf builds itself (an INSERT that cannot be transformed, a proxy entity's graphics). Non-uniformly scaled
  arcs and circles become ellipses with no source handle: `~<index>`. Keys are unique on all six drawings and on the
  synthetic ones (`test_u2_read.py`).
* **U0-7, unreadable drawings.** `read.ReadFailed` (a `RuntimeError`) carries a plain sentence that names the upload and
  never a path: "<name>: a layer name the reader cannot take (Invalid value I-FURN<x> for attribute "layer" ...); export
  the drawing again with plain layer names"; "<name>: the drawing cannot be read as a DXF (...)"; "<name>: the drawing has
  nothing in model space to read (an empty or cut-short file?)" (an empty or truncated DXF reads as an empty document
  in ezdxf, so 0 items is refused); "<name>: its blocks are nested too deeply to read" (a RecursionError, re-raised from
  the walk as U-3.5 asks, turned into ReadFailed by `read_source`). **For U1:** map `ReadFailed` to `read_failed` with
  its message. `MemoryError` propagates unchanged (the step child exits 75).

## 2. Measured values that differ from the design's text (no code change asked)

* **House level tags: 26, not 28** (U-7.3, ACCEPTANCE A-4, 2.1 U2). There are 28 level marks (18 on `SPEC`, 10 on layer
  `0`), but two of the ten on layer `0` are ATTRIBs tagged LEVEL whose text is blank. `LevelTag.value` is a number and a
  blank mark says no level, so they are left out: **26 tags, 18 on SPEC and 8 on layer 0** (the scaffold's provisional
  fixture had the same 26). The test asserts the 26 and that the 2 blank marks exist in the cache. Suggest the design
  reads "28 marks, 26 with a value".
* **Door votes count mirrored doors** (U-7.4, A-4): the reader puts an arc's centre in world coordinates (an arc inside a
  mirrored block has its extrusion turned over, and its OCS centre is not its hinge). The 4 Nov measurement used the
  OCS centre; recomputed that way the reader gives exactly 22 mm / 4 m for the house and 7 for the suite. With world
  centres: **house 28 mm, 4 m (still `votes-win`, mm); suite 10 mm**; bank1 16, bank2 31, L29 19, L30 17, unchanged.
  Every verdict and `used` is as the design says.
* **"The house 2,267 items with no points"** (U-9.1) is the count of the house's items outside both plan boxes
  (33,051 - 30,784 in the reference manifest), not of items without points: **none of the six drawings has an item
  without points**. For U3: the "no points, no floor" rule is still right, it just never fires on these six.
* **Plan geometry sizes** (U-4.3, U-7.5), gzipped with `sig` / `rsig` / `eid`: bank1 129 KB, bank2 459 KB, house 500 KB,
  suite 177 KB, L29 316 KB, L30 285 KB (the design guessed about three times 32 - 130 KB; bank2 is nearer ten times its
  48 KB: 23,497 entries of short hex strings).
* **Read times and memory** (A-10, should): standalone reads of bank1 2.1 s and bank2 5.7 s, the house 7 - 9 s, L29 3 s,
  L30 4 s, the suite 37 - 43 s (32 s of it ezdxf's recover); the suite's read peaks at 822 MB committed. Under the load
  of the other builders' tests the same reads took up to 2 - 4 times longer. The read is well inside the 150 s / 1.5 GB
  budgets.

## 3. How U2 read loose spots of the design (information; accepted unless UI says otherwise)

* **`S-GRID-IDEN` is a grid layer, not room names.** U-8.3's table puts `*-IDEN` (room names) before `S-GRID*`, so taken
  literally `S-GRID-IDEN` (the bank's grid bubbles) would be suggested `room-name` and the grid labels would become rooms.
  F16 already added the door / window tag layers before `*-IDEN` for the same reason; `suggest.py` leaves names that
  contain GRID or AXIS out of `*-IDEN`, so they reach the grid patterns. The bank's agreement with `bank_project.json`
  is 26 of 31 layers (84 %, test >= 80 %).
* **`A-AREA` / `DEFPOINTS` "with 3+ closed outlines over 2 m2"**: DrawingRead has no area per outline, so the condition
  is 3+ closed shapes and 17 m of line work (three 2 m2 squares' perimeters). The suite's `Defpoints` (10 outlines) is
  suggested `room-outline`; the bank's empty `Defpoints` is `other`.
* **Contents rules, made usable on the real drawings** (U-8.3 gives them without thresholds): fills and wall-like pairs
  3+ each; glass-like pairs 5+, no fills, their lines most of the layer's lines; door swings with leaves 2+; level tags
  half a layer's texts; room names need texts with two letters or more that are not tags (numbers are not names).
  Layer `0` gets no contents role (the house's whole upper floor is an xref on it: 2,165 arcs and 2,554 lines; the
  contents would have called it `level`). Only the evidence that tells roles apart (dimensions, level tags, door swings,
  texts, grid bubbles) is quoted against a name hit; line pairs and fills argued with every stair, railing and cabinet
  layer. The design's own example of a contradiction (the house's `RNAME`, "41 texts are door tags D1 ... D23") does not
  occur in this drawing as read: its `RNAME` holds 9 Thai level labels and 8 INSERTs. The rule is tested on a
  synthetic `RNAME` with 41 tags: "named like a room-name layer, but its 41 texts are door or window tags D1 ... W18".
* **`LayerRead.byType` also counts `DIMENSION`** (dimensions are not items, so `items` does not count them): the only
  way the suggestions can tell "only DIMENSION -> ignore" from the DrawingRead alone (`counts.dimensions` is the whole
  drawing's).
* **Area proposals: groups whose box lies 30 % or more inside a larger group's box join it.** The reference's points-only
  sampling splits the house's ground plan (a 92 % piece and an overlapping sliver) and leaves the grid bubbles above each
  plan as stacked boxes; merged, the house has 5 proposals (two plans covering regions.json's boxes 100 %, two
  elevations, one small piece between the plans) instead of 15, and the proposal makes two floors, not three. At most
  200 proposals are written (most items first). `looks`: plan = 2+ door-size arcs and wall-layer items; elevation = 2+
  level tags, or mostly long horizontal lines and no door arcs.
* **The house's grid lines are 1.5 m ticks inside its `_GRID` bubble blocks** (U-7.3, U-9.4; for U3 and UI). Taken by the
  rule (the longest grid-layer line with an end within 1.0 m of the label, or passing within 0.3 m), 36 of the 63 labels
  get a line, and only 3 of those lines (H, H1, H2, whose ticks stand inside the ground plan's box) reach a plan's box
  (the numbered ticks stop 2 - 2.5 m above it). 27 labels get none: on each plan's left 7 lettered bubbles (A B C G K L
  M) are bare texts with no tick, the 12 elevation labels' ticks lie 1.3 m from their texts, and the upper plan's `1`
  is 1.9 m from its dog-legged tick.
  So U-9.4's "a label belongs to floor i when its line crosses floor i's area" holds only if the line is read as the
  ray from the bubble along `direction` (`line[0]` is the end nearer the label, `direction` points away from it). Read
  that way, the numbered labels 2 - 12 enter one plan each and their lines lie 74.021 m apart in x (within 1 mm), the
  lettered D E F H J ticks lie at the same y on both sides (dy = 0), and the elevations' labels reach no plan
  (`test_house_grid_lines_give_the_shift_between_its_plans`). The ticks left of the ground plan point east across BOTH
  plans: U3 needs a rule for that (the first area the ray enters, say). "12 numbered and 12 lettered labels on each
  plan" (A-4) cannot come from lines in this drawing. The bank's 10 labels sit on real grid lines (> 10 m long).
* **Element id and `rsig` of an ATTRIB**: an ATTRIB belongs to its INSERT, so its block chain is its path plus its
  INSERT's block (`block`), as an INSERT's is its path plus its own name. A-4 asks that every plan entry's
  `sig` / `rsig` / `eid` equal what the builder computes from the cache item: **U3's matcher should call
  `plangeom.ref_of(item, inserts_by_key, k)` (or `plangeom.refs(items, k)`)**, the reader's own function, rather than a
  second implementation. A signature that cannot be made (a NaN or infinite coordinate: `keys.py` hashes strict JSON
  and raises) is `''`; such an item has no plan entry.
* **The proposal's "plain-sentence note that these are guesses"** (U-3.3) has no field in BuildSettings:
  `propose.notes(settings, drawings)` returns the sentences ("The floors are a guess: 2 plans were found side by side on
  one sheet; ...", "<file>: the file says inches, its doors say millimetres ...; Confirm its units on the Files step.").
  **For U1:** log them as `info` messages of the prepare run after `propose_settings`.
* **A new version of a seeded project** also takes its latest model's levels and wall heights for the floors it maps
  (when they still rise), besides the keys, names, naming and offset U-3.3 lists: a new bank version proposes L1 / L2
  at 0 / 3.70 m with walls of 3.70 m and offset [1.212, 0.348], the house ground / upper at 0.90 / 4.80 with 3.90 /
  3.50. Floors beyond the model's get the next free `L<n>` and continue upwards. A `.ms` name that does not match
  `^[A-Za-z0-9_.-]{1,60}\.ms$` is not taken.
* **`exportedFrom`**: kept from the spec's settings; when they give none, `Revit` when at least 20 block names, and 30 %
  of them, carry a Revit element id (the bank and the tower), else null (the house, the suite).
* **The spec's revision-0 settings** (whatever the API writes from meta.json and project-settings.json) give the
  proposal its office, project names and naming, defaults, furniture, vocabulary, options (a pinned offset) and fixes;
  missing parts get U-10.4's defaults, and a missing naming is derived as U-13.3 says for createProject.
* **Units decided before the walk** (U-7.4): `units.quick` votes on every block definition and model space as drawn (one
  vote per definition), so the flattening distance is right first time on the house (declared inches, doors in mm).
  The full vote then runs on the walked items; a second walk happens only when they disagree (tested with door blocks
  drawn in metres and inserted 1000 times larger). With `units` given (a units change), the read is made in them and
  the verdict still says what the file and its doors say ("...; read in centimetres as chosen").
* **Plan rings**: a closed polyline or hatch loop is simplified closed and written without its repeated last point (the
  scaffold's fixture convention, which U7's tests were written against); a ring that simplifies to fewer than two points
  keeps its points.
* **Texts and level tags in drawing.json**: blank texts are left out of `texts` and are never a level tag's label;
  `levelTags` are sorted by value (the Floors step lists them by value).
* **`reader.peakMemoryMB`** is the reading process's peak commit (PeakPagefileUsage): in the worker the read is a child
  of its own, so it is the read's. In the test process it accumulates over the reads.
* **drawing.json and plan.json** are written as compact JSON (no indentation; NaN refused).
